2. PROPOSED EDUCATIONAL VISION

Working name: Immersive Health Professions Learning Laboratory (IHPLL).

Create a shared environment connecting foundational science to clinical
practice. The organizing teaching sequence proposed in the chat is:

See it -> Explore it -> Practice it -> Apply it -> Explain it.

Relevant foundational subjects include anatomy and physiology, biology,
microbiology, chemistry, physics, pathophysiology, and pharmacology.

The intended access model is no required student purchase for laboratory
learning materials and platforms. Institutional subscriptions can support
that objective even when the software itself is commercial. The desired
procurement conditions include no student credit cards, no reliance on
expiring personal trials, and access for the entire course through an
institutionally appropriate authentication method.

The prior chat suggested that a $10,000-$30,000 institutional subscription
might be reasonable if it replaced $150-$300 per student in existing
materials. These are illustrative assumptions, not established IU costs
or savings. Any replacement and savings claim needs course-level evidence.

4. PROPOSED ROOM MODEL AND LEARNING FUNCTIONS

The earlier anatomy discussion proposed a hybrid room for roughly 24
learners, with 8-12 VR stations and teams of two or three rotating between
headsets, 2D references, and imaging. These are planning assumptions, not
confirmed enrollment or room-capacity figures.

An example task is to locate and trace the vagus nerve in VR, compare its
path with an atlas and CT/MRI slices, and explain the relationship to the
class using the large display.

The expanded lab concept proposed approximately 2,000-3,000 square feet
and the following functions. Actual capacity depends on the leased space,
clearances, access requirements, electrical service, and approved design.

A. Immersive anatomy and foundational science
- 8-12 headsets and a large instructor display.
- Virtual dissection, organ-system isolation, transparency, gross-to-micro
  exploration, normal/pathological comparisons, and formative assessment.
- Dynamic physiology and connections to radiographic anatomy.

B. Medical imaging exploration
- High-resolution displays and DICOM viewing workstations.
- Properly sourced, deidentified or synthetic CT/MRI and other teaching cases.
- Sectional anatomy, 3D reconstruction, ultrasound training, and virtual
  positioning exercises.
- Cross-program sequence: anatomy -> X-ray -> CT -> MRI -> ultrasound
  -> pathology.
- The source suggested “diagnostic-quality or high-resolution” displays.
  The required educational specification and any clinical use need to be
  defined before budgeting; those categories are not interchangeable.

C. Virtual patients and clinical reasoning
- History taking, assessment, clinical judgment, medication reasoning,
  patient education, behavioral health, emergencies, and team communication.
- Selective commercial simulation licenses plus faculty-developed activities.

D. AI learning studio
- An institutionally approved AI environment for simulated patient
  interviews, anatomy/physiology tutoring, Socratic questioning, terminology,
  handoffs, educational differential diagnosis exercises, simulated lab
  interpretation, and patient-education practice.
- Example case: a fictional 62-year-old patient with shortness of breath
  who reveals information as the student asks appropriate questions.
- Faculty-designed formative feedback and human review of assessment.
- Fictional or properly deidentified cases within applicable IU privacy,
  security, accessibility, and academic-integrity requirements.
- AI should not independently determine student competency or progression.

E. Physical clinical skills and simulation
- Start with moderate-fidelity equipment linked to the virtual activities.
- Potential capabilities: a patient-care bed station, vital signs,
  medication administration, IV/injection practice, catheterization,
  wound care, airway practice, CPR, phlebotomy, infection control,
  oral-care/dental head simulation, ultrasound, and safe patient mobility.
- Inventory existing IU resources before purchasing or adding functions.
- A high-fidelity adult simulator was suggested for a later phase. The
  chat cited approximately $84,000 for a SimMan 3G PLUS in an unspecified
  procurement document; this is not a verified quote or complete cost.

F. Shared immersive teaching and debriefing
- One approximately 98-inch or larger primary 4K display or LED wall.
- Two secondary displays, wireless presentation, instructor touchscreen,
  microphones/cameras, speakers, flexible furniture, writable surfaces,
  and controllable lighting.
- Three-wall immersive projection was proposed as a later enhancement.
- Suggested scenes included hospital rooms, emergency care, patient homes,
  dental clinics, radiology suites, and community-health settings.


5. EXAMPLE INTERDISCIPLINARY MODULE: SHORTNESS OF BREATH

Anatomy and physiology: explore the lungs, heart, and pulmonary circulation.
Microbiology: examine organisms and mechanisms associated with pneumonia.
Chemistry: connect gas transport to acid-base balance.
Physics: investigate X-ray production and attenuation.
Medical imaging: interpret instructional chest radiographs and CT examples.
Nursing: assess the simulated patient and prioritize appropriate responses.
Dental hygiene: consider oral-systemic relationships, medications, and
implications for dental care.
Health sciences: address prevention, epidemiology, and population health.
AI practice: interview a fictional patient using a faculty-designed case.
Team simulation: respond to deterioration and practice communication.
Debrief: explain the science behind decisions and identify learning gaps.

This is an illustrative teaching scenario, not a validated clinical protocol
or a completed module. Program faculty must define scope and objectives.

10. PROPOSED IMPLEMENTATION AND EVALUATION

Original phase sequence, subject to stakeholder review:

Months 0-6: prepare the space, acquire core technology, select software,
hire or assign staff, and develop faculty pilots. The chat proposed starting
with anatomy and physiology, nursing, and medical imaging.

Months 6-12: expand dental hygiene, microbiology, pathophysiology,
pharmacology, and health sciences activities; develop 10-15 interdisciplinary
modules. That module count is a proposal, not a funded commitment.

Year 2: consider high-fidelity simulation, advanced ultrasound, immersive
projection, expanded AI patient work, and faculty/student research.

The phased language is inconsistent about when AI virtual patients begin:
AI is included in the startup vision but also named as a Year 2 addition.
Research should distinguish a limited launch pilot from later expansion.
Dental hygiene and health sciences should participate in planning from the
start even if their equipment or modules are phased later.

Proposed measures:
- Course success and progression in foundational sciences.
- Anatomy practical performance and clinical reasoning.
- Faculty-assessed skills and clinical readiness.
- Retention, student confidence, and perceived learning value.
- Faculty adoption, lab utilization, and programs served.
- Interdisciplinary encounters and instructional-material costs.
- Research, scholarship, external support, and partnerships.

Retention improvement is a project objective, not an established effect
of the proposed technologies. Define baseline measures, comparison groups
where feasible, access patterns, and follow-up periods before claiming
an educational or retention benefit.

Longer-term concept: a regional center joining education, simulation, AI,
research, and workforce development, potentially partnering with hospitals,
dental practices, imaging organizations, community colleges, high schools,
and regional healthcare employers. No such partnerships were confirmed.
