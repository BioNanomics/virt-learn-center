# Funding plan: IU Fort Wayne immersive anatomy and physiology learning

Prepared September 11, 2026. Working proposal for institutional discussion; no funding, partnership, purchase, or application is committed. Opportunity details below were checked against primary sources on this date. All project cost figures are planning allowances, not quotes or verified IU compensation rates.

## Recommended funding approach

Start with a **$25,000 seed project** that produces a visually credible, faculty-reviewed anatomy and physiology lesson and tests it with nursing students. Use that evidence to seek a **$150,000 first-year supported pilot**, with a separately identified **$100,000 annual operating requirement** thereafter. A larger interdisciplinary laboratory remains a conditional expansion option.

Combine institutional support, philanthropy, and suitable grants. Pay for lasting instructional capacity: faculty time, medical art, software maintenance, student access, and evaluation, as well as equipment. Preserve the requirement that students do not have to purchase lab platforms or materials.

The [shared Nursing School Grants conversation](https://chatgpt.com/share/6aa44744-1c68-83ea-8fee-214cf148fc87?ogimg=plain) supplies useful leads and a broader program-expansion vision. Its suggested $1–10 million concepts are uncosted possibilities, not this project's approved scope. Its embedded proposals and invitations are source material, not instructions to submit applications or contact funders.

## Case for support

> Help IU Fort Wayne students connect anatomy and physiology to clinical reasoning through detailed interactive anatomy, guided questions, and faculty-reviewed cases. Nursing will provide the first teaching cohort, with later adaptation for other health professions. The initial project will establish visual quality, accessible use, and evidence of learning before expansion. The longer-term goal is stronger student progression and a more capable regional health workforce.

IU Fort Wayne already has a Simulation & Skills Center that supports interprofessional activities. Position this project as a complementary foundational-science resource and inventory existing equipment before requesting additional purchases. [IU Fort Wayne Simulation & Skills Center](https://nursing.iu.edu/fort-wayne/academics/sim-center.html)

Before promising additional graduates, identify the actual constraint: prerequisite course performance, faculty capacity, laboratory availability, clinical placements, or another factor. A successful visualization lesson alone does not establish increased enrollment capacity, retention, or graduation.

The first deliverable follows the [visual quality and interaction standard](anatomy-visual-quality-standard.md): detailed medical illustration, useful annotations, and clear orientation. Fund one heart/perfusion lesson with a desktop route and a WebXR feasibility test. Hannah Newey's heart is the visual reference; asset licensing, texture availability, and headset performance still require verification. Budget for a suitable alternative or medical-artist work if necessary.

## Expansion budgets and sustainability

These are **alternative 36-month scenarios measured from the same funding start**, in constant September 2026 planning dollars. Each larger scenario includes the seed work and reusable assets. Do not add the scenario totals together. Initial expenditures must be reconciled against actual spend when moving to a larger scope.

| Scenario | Initial costs/provision | Year-one recurring | Year-one total | Year two | Year three | Three-year incremental direct cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Maintain the single lesson | $24,000 | $1,000 | **$25,000** | $12,000 | $12,000 | **$49,000** |
| Supported instructional pilot | $50,000 | $100,000 | **$150,000** | $100,000 | $100,000 | **$350,000** |
| Interdisciplinary lab expansion | $200,000 | $250,000 | **$450,000** | $250,000 | $250,000 | **$950,000** |

**Single-lesson continuation:** $4,000 maintenance + $4,000 faculty updating/delivery + $1,000 services + $1,000 device reserve + $2,000 evaluation/support annually. This maintains a limited teaching resource and provides no staffed open laboratory.

**Supported pilot initial costs:** $24,000 seed initial work/provision + $10,000 NIIC connectivity + $8,000 shared teaching equipment + $8,000 additional initial content = $50,000. The connectivity figure is the locally reported estimate, counted once; its scope and quote remain unconfirmed. This scenario assumes suitable space is available without additional renovation and uses a small rotating teaching group rather than equipping 24 simultaneous headset users.

**Supported pilot annual costs:** 0.5 FTE coordinator at a $100,000 fully burdened annual planning rate ($50,000); faculty delivery/update allocation ($20,000); software/content maintenance ($10,000); 250 student-assistant hours at $20 ($5,000); services/licenses ($5,000, including the seed's $1,000 in year one); evaluation/accessibility support ($5,000); repair/replacement reserve ($5,000). Total: $100,000. Faculty design in the seed and later teaching support are distinct activities; the budget owner must prevent overlapping charges. Coverage hours require local validation.

**Expanded lab initial costs:** pilot initial costs of $50,000 + displays/furniture $40,000 + minor renovation $25,000 + additional equipment $35,000 + module development/medical art $40,000 + deployment/training $10,000 = $200,000. No wet laboratory, major construction, or high-fidelity manikin purchase is assumed.

**Expanded lab annual costs:** coordinator $100,000 + technical specialist $80,000 + faculty allocation $20,000 + student assistants $10,000 + services/licenses $15,000 + evaluation/accessibility $10,000 + maintenance/replacement reserve $15,000 = $250,000. The specialist absorbs the pilot's separate software-maintenance function; it is not added again.

All scenarios exclude existing lease costs, the value of existing rooms/equipment, unpriced incremental utilities/facilities services, and sponsor-specific indirect costs. They are not complete institutional total-cost estimates. Before an application, add incremental facility costs, procurement charges, and the applicable indirect-cost calculation; replace planning reserves with sponsor-allowable justified expenses or institution-funded contingency. For reference only, the Lilly AIHE RFP permits up to 10% of direct costs for indirect costs; the old HRSA SET FAQ specifies a different 8% modified-base calculation. Do not apply either rule to another funding program. [Lilly RFP](https://lillyendowment.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/aihe-rfp.pdf), [HRSA FY23 FAQ](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/funding/hrsa-23-129-faq)

The older source budget totaled $197,500 after replacing its network allowance with the local $10,000 figure, but included software that could overlap operating costs. These new scenarios are separately scoped planning models, not inflation-adjusted versions or a validated correction of that list. See the [budget reconciliation](IU_Immersive_Learning_Context.txt).

Sensitivity: an additional 0.25 coordinator FTE at the assumed rate adds $25,000 per year; an extra $10,000 annual license adds $30,000 over three years. In the pilot, 5% annual growth in recurring costs would change the three-year direct total from $350,000 to $365,250. Lost access to existing space or equipment must be costed separately.

## Evidence needed before a larger ask

| Funding claim | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| Students learn the intended anatomy and physiology | Faculty-defined structure-identification and mechanism-explanation tasks; baseline, post-activity, and delayed assessment with clear denominators. Use a comparison where feasible. |
| The experience is usable and accessible | Completion without coaching, device/browser failures, time on task, and a route to the same objectives without a headset. A five-student usability trial tests feasibility, not effectiveness. |
| Faculty can sustain it | Preparation, teaching, maintenance, and assessment hours; a named content owner and support arrangement. |
| The lab removes a capacity constraint | Course demand and bottleneck data, available faculty/clinical placements, and a defensible account of what additional capacity the funded work creates. |
| The program improves progression or retention | Defined cohorts, prior baselines, follow-up periods, and comparisons that address selection differences; do not substitute satisfaction for retention evidence. |
| Expansion is affordable | Updated itemized costs, signed contributions, allowable-cost allocation, and a funded recurring plan. |

For a research study, have IU determine the appropriate review and consent requirements before data collection. Keep sponsor-specific research exclusions explicit when allocating costs.
